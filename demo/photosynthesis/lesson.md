## Introduction to Photosynthesis  *(5 min)*

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae, and cyanobacteria use sunlight to convert light energy into chemical energy.

**Example:** Plants perform photosynthesis, releasing oxygen as a byproduct when they split water.

## Inputs and Outputs of Photosynthesis  *(8 min)*

**Definition:** Carbon fixation is the process where carbon dioxide is converted into sugars.

Photosynthesis captures energy from sunlight to convert carbon dioxide into carbohydrates. In oxygenic photosynthesis, water is used as an electron donor, releasing oxygen.

**Example:** Organic compounds stored include carbohydrates like sugars (glucose, fructose, sucrose), starches, and cellulose.

## Role of Chlorophyll and Sunlight  *(8 min)*

**Definition:** Chlorophyll is any of several related photochemical catalysts and green pigments found in cyanobacteria and in the chloroplasts of algae and plants.

Chlorophyll allows plants to absorb energy from light. Special chlorophyll molecules absorb the energy of photons, promoting electrons to a higher energy state.

**Example:** In light-dependent reactions, chlorophyll absorbs one photon and loses an electron, which starts an electron transport chain.

## Examining Photosynthetic Energy Conversion  *(4 min)*

**Example:** When sunlight strikes chlorophyll, the green pigment absorbs light energy, allowing plants to capture energy from the sun while water molecules are split to help drive the process.
